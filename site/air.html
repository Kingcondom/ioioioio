AIR-MCQ-02MCQMassive hemoptysis· AIR-01 Approach to dyspnea, cough and hemoptysis

A 55-year-old man with old pulmonary tuberculosis coughs up 500 mL of fresh blood within 6 hours. PE: T 37.2 C, PR 118/min, RR 30/min, BP 100/60 mmHg, SpO2 (room air) 88%; crepitation over the RIGHT upper lung, clear left lung. Which of the following is the MOST appropriate immediate management?

  1. Start empirical anti-tuberculous drugs and arrange an outpatient CT chest
  2. Start intravenous tranexamic acid and observe in the emergency room
  3. Place the patient in the left lateral decubitus position to drain the bleeding lung

Pearl — In massive hemoptysis, death is from asphyxiation, not exsanguination. Bleeding side DOWN protects the good lung, then secure the airway, then localise and embolize.

AIR-MCQ-03MCQMechanism of dyspnea· AIR-01 Approach to dyspnea, cough and hemoptysis

PE: T 37.0 C, PR 90/min, RR 24/min, BP 130/80 mmHg, SpO2 (room air) 96%, jugular venous pressure 6 cm above the sternal angle, loud P2, no murmur, no distant heart sounds, pitting edema of both legs 3+. AIR-MCQ-04MCQSpirometry pattern· AIR-02 Pulmonary function test & spirometry interpretation

  3. Morbid obesity
  4. Asthma

เฉลย: d. Asthma
Pearl — FEV1/FVC below 70% = obstructive pattern; only asthma on this list is obstructive. Obesity, effusion, atelectasis and consolidation all produce a restrictive pattern (low FVC, preserved ratio).
