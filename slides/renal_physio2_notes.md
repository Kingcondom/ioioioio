## 1. Body fluid volume & osmolality (p.3–26)
- p.4 intake = output 2.5 L/day. Regulation: (1) water shift between compartments (2) renal control of Na & water (sympathetic, hormones).
- p.5 Water moves by osmotic pressure difference ECF↔ICF. ↑ECF osm → water out of cell → cell shrinking; ↓ECF osm → cell swelling.
  Posm = 2[Na] + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8. Urea crosses membranes → no effect on water movement. Effective osm (tonicity) = 2[Na] + glucose/18.
- p.6 Volume deficit (Darrow–Yannet): isosmotic contraction (diarrhea, vomiting, burn, hemorrhage) → no shift → Rx isotonic 0.9% NaCl ·
  hyperosmotic contraction (sweating, water deprivation, fever, alcoholism, DI) → water out of cells → Rx hypotonic 0.45% NaCl, D5W ·
  hyposmotic contraction (adrenal insufficiency/hypoaldosteronism; solute loss > water) → water into cells → Rx hypertonic 3% NaCl.
  ✍️ hyposmotic contraction: adrenal gland ไม่หลั่ง aldosterone → เสีย Na มากกว่าน้ำ
- p.7 Volume excess: isosmotic expansion (0.9% NaCl infusion) · hyperosmotic expansion (high NaCl intake, 3% NaCl) → cell dehydration · hyposmotic expansion (excess water drinking, SIADH) → cell swelling.
- p.8 Hct = RBC volume (ICF)/blood volume ×100; Table 6.2 (Costanzo): blood vol / Posm / RBC vol / Hct / plasma protein for six states.
  Iso contraction ↓ NC NC ↑ ↑ · Hyperosm contraction ↓ ↑ ↓ NC ↑ · Hyposm contraction ↓ ↓ ↑ ↑ ↑ · Iso expansion ↑ NC NC ↓ ↓ · Hyperosm expansion ↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ↓ · Hyposm expansion (SIADH) ↑ ↓ ↑ NC ↓
- p.9–10 Renal regulation via filtration (autoregulation MAP 80–170 keeps filtered load; dehydration → sympathetic + AII → ↓RBF ↓GFR) and reabsorption
  (glomerulotubular balance; sympathetic ↑ Na reabs at PT, TAL, DT, CD; hormones AII, epinephrine, aldosterone, ADH, ANP; water: GT balance, medullary gradient, ADH).
- p.11 GT balance: ↓BV → ↑sympathetic & AII → ↓GFR/↓↓RPF → ↑FF → ↓Pc ↑πc → ↑proximal Na reabs. ↑BV → ↓FF → pressure natriuresis.
  Nephron % Na reabsorbed: Normal PT 65, TAL 25, DT 5, CD 3, excr <1 · Low BP PT 80, DT 3, TAL 15, CD 2, excr 0 · High BP PT 50, DT 12, TAL 30, CD 2, excr 6.
- p.12 Sympathetic: ↓BV/BP → CV center → renal arteriole constriction (↓RBF), mesangial contraction (↓GC area → ↓GFR), JG renin ↑ → RAAS, ↑ tubular Na reabs → ↓NaCl & H2O excretion. ANP & high BV/BP inhibit.
- p.13 RAAS (Sherwood diagram): AII → arteriolar vasoconstriction, CV center, hypothalamus (ADH, thirst), adrenal cortex (aldosterone), kidney (efferent constriction).
- p.14 Aldosterone stimuli: ↓plasma Na, ↓BV/BP → sympathetic → renin → AII; volume loss; ↑plasma K. Inhibitors: very high osmolality, ANP, high Na diet. Zona glomerulosa → late DT/CD: P cell ↑Na reabs ↑K secretion; type A-IC ↑H secretion.
- p.15 ADH: ↑effective plasma osmolality (osmoreceptor, hypothalamus) +, ↓ECV via volume receptor (atrium/lung) +, ↓BP baroreceptor (aortic/carotid) +; morphine, nicotine +; ANP, alcohol −. Posterior pituitary → P cell late DT/CD ↑water reabs. High dose → V1R vasoconstriction.
  ✍️ "Effective plasma osmolality" — urea ไม่กระตุ้น ADH.
- p.16 ADH actions: ↑H2O permeability late DT/CD; ↑NaCl reabs TAL (↑ medullary gradient); ↑urea reabs inner medullary CD. Posm normal 285–295; Posm < 275 → no ADH secretion.
- p.17 Thirst: ↑osm, ↓atrial volume, ↓BP baroreceptor, ↑AII, dry mouth → thirst center; gastric distension by fluid intake inhibits (✍️).
- p.18 ANP: atrial stretch → ↓sympathetic (CV center), ↓ADH, ↓aldosterone, kidney: ↓renin/AII, afferent vasodilation ↑RBF, mesangial (GMG) relaxation ↑GFR, ↓Na reabs at P cell → ↑NaCl & H2O excretion.
- p.19 Integrated flowchart: hyperosmotic volume contraction (water deprivation/sweating) — ↑ADH, thirst, sympathetic, renin/AII, aldosterone, ↑FF ↓Pc ↑πc → ↑PT reabs; ↑H secretion/HCO3 reabs → contraction alkalosis; ↓FENa; ↑urea reabs → ↑BUN, ↑BUN/Cr; ↓CH2O; Uosm/Posm >1 antidiuresis.
- p.20–21 Volume contraction causes (inadequate intake, GI, renal [diuretic, osmotic diuresis, Addison], skin [fever, sweat, burn], blood loss, third space [edema, ascites]); clinical: wt loss mild 2% moderate 2–5% severe ≥8%; postural hypotension, tachycardia, flat neck veins; sunken fontanel/eyes; thirst, dry mouth, poor turgor; ↓urine vol ↑Uosm; early fever.
- p.22 Hyposmotic volume expansion (excess water drinking) flowchart → ↓ADH, ↑ANP, ↓sympathetic/renin/AII/aldo → water diuresis, Uosm/Posm < 1, ↑CH2O, solute wash-out.
- p.23–24 Volume expansion causes (CHF, renal failure, ↑corticosteroids [hyperaldosteronism, Cushing], cirrhosis; excess Na intake/IV; excess fluid) · clinical: wt gain 2/5/≥8%, bounding pulse, JVD, pulmonary edema (SOB, crackles), dependent/generalized edema.
- p.25 Salty diet → hyperosmotic volume expansion: ↑ADH + thirst (water returns osm to normal) + ↑BV → ↑ANP ↓RAAS ↓sympathetic → ↑NaCl & water excretion (slow).
- p.26 Exercise: sweating + mouth breathing (insensible loss) → water loss > solute → ↑ADH, thirst; sympathetic → renin/AII/aldo → antidiuresis.

## 2. Electrolytes (p.27–67)
- p.28 Ionic composition plasma/interstitial/ICF; balance intake = output, kidney main output.
- p.29–30 Na: ↑Na intake → ↑ECF/EABV → ↓sympathetic, ↑ANP, ↓πc, ↓RAAS → ↑Na excretion (and reverse for ↓intake). ✍️ Na balance = water balance; hormones RAA + sympathetic.
- p.31–32 K: [K]ECF + leak K efflux determine RMP. Hyper K early: depolarized RMP → ↑excitability → twitching/cramps; late: Na channel inactivation → weakness/flaccid. Hypo K: hyperpolarization → ↓excitability → weakness.
- p.33–34 Cardiac: hyperK (mild 5.5–6.5, moderate 6.5–8, severe >8): ↑phase 3 K efflux → tall peaked T (VT/VF); depolarized RMP → ↓phase 0 → wide flat P, prolonged PR, wide QRS, sine wave (brady, arrest). HypoK: ↓phase 3 → flat T, prominent U, ST depression, slightly peaked P.
- p.35–37 ECG examples: K 7.0 peaked T; K 9.0 rhabdomyolysis sine wave; K 1.7 ST depression, T inversion, U wave, long QU.
- p.38 K homeostasis: normal 3.5–5.0; intake 100 mmol/day; ECF 65 mmol, muscle 2600 mmol (✍️ ส่วนใหญ่อยู่ในเซลล์). Mechanisms: shift; renal.
- p.39 Shift: aldosterone, insulin, epinephrine β2 → Na/K-ATPase in muscle, bone, liver, RBC → ↓PK; α-adrenergic → ↓ATPase → ↑PK. Metabolic acidosis inhibits Na/K-ATPase + H/K exchange → ↑PK; alkalosis opposite.
- p.40 Table 5.4 shifts out (hyperK): insulin deficiency (type 1 DM), β-blockers, acidosis, hyperosmolarity (solvent drag), Na-K pump inhibitors (digitalis), exercise (↓ATP → open KATP), cell lysis (hemolysis, rhabdo). Into cells (hypoK): insulin, β-agonist (salbutamol), alkalosis, hyposmolarity.
- p.41 Renal K: 65% PT (with Na/H2O), 25% TAL; DT/CD reabsorb or secrete via P cell and A-IC. Normal/high intake: DCT 8–50% secretion, CCD 5–30% secretion, excretion 15–80%. Low intake: 2% reabs + 7% reabs, excretion 1%.
- p.42 Factors ↑ K secretion: ↑PK, ↓PMg (Mg normally blocks ROMK ✍️), ↑aldosterone (JG tumor, adrenal tumor), ↑distal Na delivery & flow, ↑luminal anion, metabolic alkalosis.
- p.43 Flow: ↑flow bends primary cilia (PKD1/PKD2) → Ca influx → BK (Ca-sensitive K) channel → ↑K secretion; also ↓tubular [K] keeps gradient. ↑Na delivery → ↑ENaC Na entry → Na/K-ATPase → ↑cell K → ↑secretion. Diuretics (except K-sparing), high Na diet, volume expansion.
- p.44 Luminal anions: CA inhibitor (HCO3) · vomiting → metabolic alkalosis → filtered HCO3 > reabsorptive capacity → lumen negative → ↑K secretion.
- p.45 Acute metabolic alkalosis: P cell ↑Na/K-ATPase, ↑K channel open; B-IC ↑H-ATPase, H/K-ATPase → ↑K secretion → hypoK. Acute metabolic acidosis → ↓K secretion → hyperK.
- p.46 Table 5.5: ↑distal K secretion — high K diet, hyperaldosteronism, alkalosis, thiazide, loop, luminal anions; ↓ — low K diet, hypoaldo, acidosis, K-sparing diuretics.
- p.47 Acute vs chronic met acidosis (Berne-Levy fig 36.10): chronic → ↓PT NaCl/H2O reabs → ↑flow + ↓ECV → aldosterone → ↑K secretion. ✍️ not tested.
- p.48 Ca: total body 1,300 g; 99% bone, 1% ICF, 0.1% ECF. Plasma: ionized 50%, complexed 10%, protein-bound 40% (albumin ~90%). ↑phosphate → ↑complexed → ↓iCa; ↓albumin → ↑ionized (fraction); acidemia → H displaces Ca from albumin → ↑iCa.
- p.49–51 iCa sets threshold of voltage-gated Na channel (normal opens at −55 mV). HypoCa → ↓threshold → hyperexcitability (numbness, tingling, hyperreflexia, cramps, tetany, carpopedal spasm; post-thyroidectomy). Chvostek (tap 2 cm anterior to ear lobe; grade 1–4; ~25% of hypoCa). Trousseau (cuff > SBP+20 mmHg × 3 min; 94% sensitive).
- p.52–53 HyperCa → ↑threshold → weakness. Cardiac: hypoCa → prolonged ST/QT; hyperCa → short ST/QT, widened T.
- p.54–57 ECG: hypoCa (post-thyroidectomy, corrected Ca 2.8? [slide typo — likely ionized]) QT 0.44; hyperparathyroid Ca 12.2 QT 0.28.
- p.58 Ca homeostasis: normal total 9–10 (<8.5 hypo, >10.5 hyper); intake 1,000 mg/d, absorption 350, secretion 250, feces 900, urine 100 mg/d; filtered 9,980 reabsorbed 9,880; bone formation/resorption 500. Regulators PTH ↑, calcitriol ↑, calcitonin ↓.
- p.59 Plasma Ca regulation flowchart (↓Ca → CaSR → PTH → bone resorption, kidney ↑Ca reabs + 1α-hydroxylase → calcitriol → gut). Calcitonin: no minute-to-minute role in humans.
- p.60 Renal Ca: 65% PT (paracellular with Na/H2O), 25% TAL, 8% early DT (PTH, calcitriol sensitive; thiazide ↑reabs), 1% CCD; excretion <1%. ↑reabs: ↑PTH, ↑calcitriol, thiazide, ↑phosphate, ↑albumin, volume contraction, alkalemia.
- p.61–64 P: total 600 g; 85% bone, 14% ICF, 1% ECF; plasma ionized 45% (HPO4:H2PO4 80:20), complexed 30%, protein-bound 25%. Normal 3–4 mg/dL (<2.5 hypo, >4.5 hyper). PTH ↓P, FGF-23 ↓P (osteocytes; ↓ NaPi + inhibits calcitriol). Renal: 80% PT (NaPi-IIa/IIc; PTH/FGF23 internalize), 10% DT; ↓reabs: ↑PTH, ↑FGF23, ↑P, ↓Ca, volume expansion, metabolic acidosis (P used as urinary buffer — titratable acid).
- p.65–67 Mg: total 25 g; bone 54%, ICF 45% (muscle 27, soft tissue 19, RBC 0.5), ECF 1%. Plasma ionized 60%, complexed 10%, protein 30%. Normal 1.5–2.3 mg/dL. HypoMg → tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau. Renal: 30% PT, 60% TAL (paracellular, driven by lumen-positive voltage; furosemide ↓), 5% DT (TRPM6; thiazide ↓). ↑reabs: ↑PTH, EGF, ↑PMg(?), volume contraction, alkalosis.

## 3. Acid-base control (p.68–79)
- p.69 Normal pH 7.35–7.45 (clinic 7.4). Acids: diet 20 mEq H/day; GI secretes OH- (leaves H) 10 mEq/day; metabolism: volatile CO2 15,000 mmol/day (lungs), fixed acid 40 mEq/day → total 70 mEq H/day excreted by kidney. Bases: HCO3 (organic anion metabolism; citrate) & NH3. Filtered HCO3 4320 mmol/day reabsorbed; new HCO3 70; NH4 40 + TA 30 mmol/day.
- p.70 Sources H: aerobic (carbonic), anaerobic (lactic), phosphoproteins/nucleic acids (phosphoric), sulfur AA (sulfuric), incomplete FA oxidation (ketone bodies).
- p.71 Three systems: buffers, respiratory, renal.
- p.72 Buffers: 1st line, instantaneous, short-term, don't eliminate acid. Blood: Hb, HCO3 (80% of plasma buffering), plasma protein 15%, Pi 5%; ICF protein/phosphate; bone; urine phosphate & NH3.
- p.73 ICF buffer; H/K exchange: acidemia → H into cell → ↑PK; alkalemia → ↓PK. NOTE: H/K exchange does NOT occur in respiratory disorders or organic acid metabolic acidosis (lactate enters with H). ✍️
- p.74 Respiratory: 2nd line, minutes–hours (max 12–24 h), corrects ~50–60%.
- p.75 Renal: 3rd line, hours–days, most powerful; filtered HCO3 reabs (PT 80%, TAL 10%, DT 6%, CD 4% ✍️), new HCO3 formation, H secretion. Plasma HCO3 > 25 → excretion (threshold curve). No net H excretion by reclamation.
- p.76 H excretion: throughout tubule, mainly late DT/CCD type A-IC. Urine min pH 4.5 (0.03 mEq/L free H → 2,667 L/day if free). Normal urine pH 6–6.5. Forms: titratable acid (NaH2PO4) 40%, NH4Cl 60%. 1 H excreted = 1 new HCO3.
- p.77 Ammoniagenesis in PT: glutamine → 2 NH4 + 2 HCO3 (slide shows 2 glutamine → 4NH4 + 4HCO3 + glucose); NH4 secreted via NHE3, reabsorbed in TAL (substitutes K on NKCC2), medullary interstitium, NH3 diffuses into CD, trapped as NH4. ↑ by acidosis & hypokalemia. NAE = TA + NH4 − HCO3 (U×V).
- p.78 ↑HCO3 reabs/H excretion: ↑PaCO2, ↑[H] (fixed acid), ↑lumen-negative potential (Na reabs at P cell), hypokalemia (↑NH3 synthesis PT, ↑H/K-ATPase A-IC), ↑AII (NHE3), ↑aldosterone (H-ATPase). ↓ECF → AII + aldo → contraction alkalosis. ✍️ diuretic → contraction alkalosis.
- p.79 Summary diagram.

## 4. Disorders & compensation (p.80–88)
- p.81 Henderson-Hasselbalch pH = 6.1 + log [HCO3]/(0.03 × PaCO2). Simple vs mixed. Primary HCO3 (metabolic) vs PaCO2 (respiratory).
- p.82 Metabolic acidosis causes table: excess fixed acid (DKA ↑AG; lactic ↑AG (hypoxia from hypoxemia or hypoperfusion); salicylate late ↑AG + resp alkalosis; methanol/formaldehyde ↑AG ↑osmolar gap; ethylene glycol ↑AG ↑osm gap). Loss of HCO3 (diarrhea NAG hyperchloremic; type 2 RTA NAG). Inability to excrete fixed H (CKD ↑AG; type 1 RTA ↓acidify urine NAG; type 4 RTA hypoaldo ↓NH4 hyperK NAG).
- p.83 Compensation met acidosis: buffers (HCO3 consumed; H displaces Ca from albumin → ↑iCa; H into cells → K out ↑PK) · respiratory ↑VT & RR (Kussmaul***) ↓PaCO2 · renal ↑HCO3 reabs/production, ↑ammoniagenesis, ↑NAE, acidic urine.
- p.84 Metabolic alkalosis causes: loss of H (vomiting — maintained by volume contraction, hypoK; hyperaldosteronism — hypoK); gain of HCO3 (NaHCO3 ingestion, milk-alkali); volume contraction alkalosis (loop/thiazide).
- p.85 Compensation alkalosis: H dissociates from albumin → ↓iCa; H out of cells → K in → ↓PK; hypoventilation ↑PaCO2; renal ↓reabs ↓NAE alkaline urine.
- p.86 Respiratory acidosis causes: medullary depression (opiates, barbiturates, anesthetics, CNS lesions, central sleep apnea, O2 therapy in CO2 retainer), muscle (GBS, polio, ALS, MS), airway obstruction (aspiration ✍️ สำลัก, OSA, laryngospasm), gas exchange (ARDS, COPD, pneumonia, pulmonary edema). Compensation: acute ICF buffer; chronic renal ↑HCO3, acidic urine.
- p.87 Respiratory alkalosis: hysterical hyperventilation, gram-negative septicemia, salicylate early, CNS disorders; hypoxemia (high altitude, pneumonia, PE); mechanical ventilation. Compensation acute ICF buffer; chronic renal ↓HCO3, alkaline urine.
- p.88 Mixed (Table 9.12): cardiopulmonary arrest (resp + lactic), post-op vomiting + hyperventilation (met alk + resp alk), renal failure + vomiting (met acid + met alk) ✍️, liver failure (resp alk + met acid), DKA + vomiting, DKA + lactic, COPD + diuretic (resp acid + met alk), septic shock + ARDS (met acid + resp acid?) [slide: lactic + resp acidosis], salicylate (resp alk + met acid).

## 6. Micturition (p.97–102)
- p.98 CNS: cerebral micturition center (prefrontal cortex — perception, permit/suppress voiding) · pontine micturition center / pontine storage center (relay; switches filling vs voiding; coordinates sphincters & bladder) · sacral micturition center S2–S4 (micturition reflex center). Sympathetic T10–L2 hypogastric.
- p.99 Sympathetic (NE, hypogastric): detrusor relaxation β3, bladder neck/internal sphincter/urethra contraction α1 → storage. Parasympathetic (ACh, pelvic): detrusor contraction M3, internal sphincter relaxation → voiding. Somatic (ACh, pudendal): external sphincter contraction (nicotinic) → voluntary. Sensory Aδ afferent pelvic nerve (distension).
- p.100 Bladder at rest: internal sphincter passively contracted, external stays contracted (tonic discharge). Reflex at volume > 150 mL: stretch → Aδ → sacral cord → parasympathetic → detrusor contracts; somatic inhibited → external sphincter relaxes; higher center can facilitate/inhibit.
- p.101 Flowchart: promotes reflex (↑parasymp, ↓symp, ↓somatic) vs pontine storage inhibits (↓parasymp, ↑symp, ↑somatic).
- p.102 Table: during filling — parasymp inhibited, symp stimulated, somatic stimulated; during micturition — reverse.
