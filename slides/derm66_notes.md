## p5–12 Primary & secondary lesions (textbook table figures, block diagrams + photos)
- Macule: flat, circumscribed, <1 cm, hypo/hyperpigmented or other colours (pink, red, violet). Ex: ephelid, lentigo, IGH, petechiae, flat viral exanthem. Photo solar lentigines.
- Patch: flat, >1 cm. Ex: vitiligo, melasma, Mongolian spot, café-au-lait, nevus depigmentosus, solar purpura.
- Papule: elevated, <1 cm; elevation from thickened epidermis and/or cells/deposits in dermis; may have secondary changes (scale, crust); distinguish from vesicle/pustule. Ex: seborrheic keratosis (✍️ "กระเนื้อ"), cherry haemangioma, nevus, molluscum, verruca, lichen nitidus, small-vessel vasculitis.
- Plaque: elevated, >1 cm. Epidermal: psoriasis (✍️ "สะเก็ดเงิน"), LSC, nummular dermatitis. Dermal: granuloma annulare, sarcoidosis, hypertrophic scar/keloid, morphea, lichen sclerosus.
- Nodule: palpable, circumscribed, "larger volume than papule" (highlighted), usually >1 cm, involves dermis and/or subcutis, greatest portion may be beneath surface. Ex: epidermoid cyst, lipoma, metastases, neurofibroma, panniculitis e.g. erythema nodosum, lymphoma cutis.
- Wheal (highlighted): transient elevation due to dermal oedema (highlighted); often pale centrally with erythematous rim. ✍️ "บวมจาก dermis" (arrow into dermis), Urticaria ✍️ "ลมพิษ". Photo acute annular urticaria.
- Vesicle: elevated, <1 cm (highlighted), clear fluid, may become pustular/umbilicated/erosion. Ex HSV, VZV, dermatitis herpetiformis, dyshidrotic eczema.
- Bulla: >1 cm (highlighted), clear fluid. Ex friction blister, bullous pemphigoid, linear IgA, bullous FDE, coma bullae, oedema bullae.
- Pustule: ✍️ "ตุ่มหนอง"; usually <1 cm, purulent fluid. Follicular: folliculitis, acne. Non-follicular: pustular psoriasis, AGEP, subcorneal pustular dermatosis.
- Secondary: Crust ✍️ "สะเก็ด" + "สะเก็ดน้ำเหลือง" (dried serum, blood or pus; may include bacteria — usually Staph). Ex eczema, impetigo, late HSV/VZV, EM.
- Scale: hyperkeratosis; accumulation of stratum corneum from increased proliferation and/or delayed desquamation. Ex psoriasis (micaceous), tinea (leading scale), EAC (trailing scale), actinic keratosis (gritty), pityriasis rosea (peripheral collarette + central).
- Fissure: linear cleft, often painful; from drying, thickening, loss of elasticity. Angular cheilitis, hand dermatitis, sebopsoriasis, irritant cheilitis.
- Erosion (highlighted "partial loss of the epidermis"): impetigo, friction, trauma, pemphigus.
- Ulceration (highlighted "full-thickness loss of the epithelium (epidermis)"): may lose dermis/subcutis. Ecthyma gangrenosum, pyoderma gangrenosum, stasis ulcer, ecthyma, neuropathic ulcer.
- Excoriation ✍️ "รอยเกา": exogenous injury to all/part of epidermis. Pruritic conditions (arthropod bites, AD), neurotic excoriations, acne excoriée.
- Atrophy ✍️ "ดูฝ่อ": epidermal (thin, wrinkled, shiny) vs dermal (loss of collagen/elastin → depression). Lichen sclerosus, poikiloderma, anetoderma, Goltz, striae (photo: striae from potent corticosteroids).
- Lichenification ✍️ "ผิวหนังหนาขึ้น สีคล้ำขึ้น, skin fissure ดูมากขึ้นชัดขึ้น": acanthosis + accentuated skin lines. LSC, AD.
- p12 Table 5-1 "The lesions of the skin": Raised (papule, plaque, nodule, cyst, wheal, scar, comedo, horn, calcinosis) · Depressed (erosion, ulcer, atrophy, poikiloderma, sinus, striae, burrow, sclerosis) · Flat (macule, patch, erythema, erythroderma) · Surface change (scale, crust, excoriation, fissure, lichenification, keratoderma, eschar) · Fluid-filled (vesicle, bulla, pustule, furuncle, abscess) · Vascular (purpura, telangiectasia, infarct).

## p17–28 Eczema / dermatitis — definition, stages, classification, cycle, treatment
- p18: eczema & dermatitis essentially the same meaning: inflammation of epidermis and dermis. Includes AD ("eczema", "winter dry skin"). All share pruritus, inflammation, disruption of skin barrier. "Inflammation of the skin characterised by itching, redness, scaling and clustered papulovesicles."
- p19 photos (eczema on ankle, toes, neck) no text.
- p20 Stages: Acute — erythema, minute papules and vesicles which may rupture → oozing and crust. Subacute — oedema and vesiculation less apparent; papules, erythema, some scales predominate. Chronic — no oozing/crusting; dry, scaly, may be fissured; repeated attacks → lichenification = thickening, hyperkeratosis, hyperpigmentation, increased skin markings.
- p21 acute (vesicles/bullae weeping), p22 subacute (scaly erythematous patches forearm), p23 chronic (lichenified, fissured).
- p24 Classification: Endogenous — atopic, seborrheic, discoid (nummular), pityriasis alba, stasis, asteatotic (xerotic) ✍️ "แห้ง", dyshidrosis (pompholyx) ✍️ "*มาเป็นตุ่มน้ำ", prurigo nodularis, LSC. Exogenous — irritant contact, allergic contact, photodermatitis.
- p25 "The eczema cycle" (ThinkSkinMD): poor barrier function (✍️ "ฟอกสบู่") → loss of water → irritants getting in (environment, bacteria) → inflammation + itchiness (✍️ "คัน") → scratching (✍️ "เกา") → worsening barrier function → …
- p26 "Breaking the eczema cycle": moisturiser (more vaseline/thicker consistency is better — "*more Vaseline, watery consistency is BETTER!"? reads "more vaseline, watery consistency is better" — ambiguous; standard teaching = thicker/greasier (ointment) better barrier) · bleach baths (kill bacteria) · steroids: "No inflammation = no itchiness = no scratching" · healing.
- p27 Treatment of eczema: correct underlying factors (avoid sensitisers/solvents, treat varicose veins, foci of infection, hot water) · broad-spectrum antibiotics if secondary infection · oral antihistamines · topical corticosteroids (highlighted) of appropriate strength for age and area, twice daily for days — creams for acute/subacute, ointments for dry chronic · emollients (vaseline) · topical immunomodulators tacrolimus 0.03% oint or pimecrolimus 1% cream bid · short systemic steroid in acute severe widespread · UV: PUVA, NB-UVB.
- p28 photo popliteal eczema. ✍️ "Atopic dermatitis" ← "eczema ตามข้อพับ".

## p29–36 Atopic dermatitis · pityriasis alba
- p29 AD: 15–20% in early childhood (✍️ "พบมากในเด็ก แต่ adult ก็เจอได้"). Major features: pruritus · eczematous dermatitis (acute/subacute/chronic) with typical morphology and age-specific patterns — facial & extensor in infancy; flexural eczema/lichenification in children & adults · chronic or chronically relapsing · personal/family history of atopy (AR, asthma, AD).
- p30 Distribution by age: Infant (birth–2 y) face (cheeks), scalp, ears, extensor extremities, seborrheic dermatitis overlap · Childhood (2 y–puberty) face (cheeks), flexural extremities · Teenager–adult localized flexural extremities, hands, dorsum feet.
- p31 infant facial AD (oozing, serous crust). p32 childhood AD lichenified antecubital fossae; AD extending from antecubital fossae to wrists/hands with excoriation & lichenification. p33 prurigo papules in AD; nipple eczema in adolescent with AD. p34 photo pityriasis alba (hypopigmented patch on cheek).
- p35 Pityriasis alba: children & adolescents with AD · multiple ill-defined hypopigmented macules/patches 0.5–2 cm with fine scale on face, shoulders, arms · following sun exposure · Rx topical steroid, sunscreens.
- p36 photo elderly face with greasy scaling on forehead/eyebrows/nasolabial (seb derm).

## p37–41 Seborrheic dermatitis
- p37: 1–3% adults (23% elderly) · chronic multifactorial (endogenous + exogenous) · inflammatory response to Malassezia yeast (highlighted). ✍️ "เด่นที่ T zone" · ✍️ "endogenous eczema" · ✍️ "นศพ. ชอบเป็น → เครียด, นอนดึก, พักผ่อนน้อย" · ✍️ "พบได้ในทุกอายุ".
- p38 Clinical: mild form = scaling limited to scalp (dandruff) ✍️ "รังแค (อาการน้อยสุดของ seb derm)" · poorly demarcated erythematous patches with greasy scaling ✍️ "เหลืองๆ" · scalp, nasolabial folds, ears, eyebrows, anterior chest, upper back. Photos eyebrows/glabella, scalp margin.
- p39 Risk factors: older age, male, obesity, stress/depression (highlighted), winter, alcohol, tobacco, HTN, HIV, neurologic disease (cerebrovascular event (highlighted) & Parkinson). Photo: bedridden elderly with NG tube — ✍️ circle "greasy scale (ก้อนมันเหลือง?)".

- p41 Treatment: lifestyle management · topical corticosteroids (✍️ star) · shampoos and topical antifungals ✍️ "รักษา Malassezia yeast" · calcineurin inhibitors.

## p67–75 Urticaria / angioedema
- p68 photo wheals. p69: urticaria = itchy well-demarcated reddish evanescent swellings (wheals) and flare · angioedema = swellings of deep dermal & subcutaneous/submucosal tissues; painful rather than itchy, poorly defined, pale or skin-coloured. Fig 38-3 urticaria face/neck/trunk + periorbital angioedema. p70 photos widespread geographic wheals trunk/thighs.
- p71 Pathophysiology: Allergic — histamine important mediator; mast cells major histamine-releasing cells; allergen + IgE bound on mast cells → degranulation → histamine & other mediators → ↑ permeability of capillaries & venules. Non-allergic — direct mast cell degranulation without Ag–Ab: aspirin, neuropeptides, NSAIDs, opiates, ciprofloxacin, polymyxin, rifampicin, vancomycin.
- p72 Causes (12): food (additives/preservatives, fish, banana, nuts, eggs, chocolate, cheese) · drugs (aspirin, NSAIDs, antibiotics e.g. penicillin) · inhalants (pollen, dust, animal fur) · intestinal parasites · stress · septic foci (teeth, tonsils, urinary tract) · physical (heat, cold, water, vibration, sunlight, pressure) · cholinergic urticaria (small wheals with sweating from heat/emotional stress) · insect bites/stings · contact urticaria (occupational) · medical (hepatitis, obstructive jaundice, H. pylori) · serum sickness.
- p73 Acute < 6 weeks (continuous or intermittent) · chronic ≥ 6 weeks; no cause found → chronic idiopathic urticaria.
- p74 Treatment: 1 treat cause · 2 anti-H1 first line — classic (chlorpheniramine, diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine); non-sedating (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine, desloratadine, acrivastine) · 3 anti-H2 may be added (cimetidine, ranitidine) · 4 systemic drug ✍️ "ให้ยากิน(?)" · 5 local calamine lotion · 6 angioedema with airway compromise (oropharyngeal-laryngeal oedema): epinephrine 1/1000 first line, "given subcutaneously in a dose __ ml" (dose blank on slide).
- p75 Chronic urticaria treatment algorithm (Beck LA et al. Acta Derm Venereol 2017;97:149-158): (A) EAACI/GA2LEN/EDF/WAO: sgAH → if inadequate after 2–4 wk ↑ sgAH up to 4× → if inadequate after 2–4 wk add omalizumab → if inadequate within 6 months add cyclosporine. (B) US practice parameter: Step 1 sgAH monotherapy + avoid triggers (NSAIDs) & physical factors · Step 2 one or more: ↑ sgAH dose, add another sgAH, add H2 antagonist, add leukotriene modifier, add first-gen AH at bedtime · Step 3 ↑ sedating first-gen AH (hydroxyzine, doxepin) · Step 4 alternative: omalizumab or cyclosporine; other anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressants, biologics.
  [Lesson note: EAACI/GA²LEN/EuroGuiDerm/APAAACI 2022 keeps the same 3-step ladder; does not recommend H2 blockers/montelukast as routine, avoid first-gen AH; systemic steroid only short course ≤10 days for exacerbation.]

## p81–92 Syphilis
- p81: T. pallidum subsp. pallidum. 1 primary (chancre) · 2 secondary (skin eruption ± LAD, organ disease) · 3 latent (no signs/symptoms, only reactive serology) · 4 tertiary (cutaneous, neurologic, cardiovascular). Figure 200-8 plantar lesions of secondary syphilis in a man who also had a penile chancre.
- p82 Natural history of untreated syphilis (Rein & Musher): inoculation → 10–90 d incubation (average 3 wk) → primary → haematogenous dissemination, 3–10 wk after chancre → secondary → after 3–12 wk lesions disappear spontaneously → latent (up to 25% relapse to secondary within first 1–2 years) → 2–20 years (highlighted) → no recurrence (cure) ✍️ "หายไปเลย" or tertiary ✍️ "→ neuro/cardio syphilis".
- p83 Fig 82.5 chancres of primary syphilis — firm to palpation, occasionally multiple; penis, perianal, lip. ✍️ "Chancres".
- p84 Secondary syphilis: prodromal symptoms · generalized LAD (50–85%) · skin: early (10%) generalized non-pruritic roseola-like discrete macules; late (70%) generalized maculopapular & papulosquamous (highlighted) eruptions; palms & soles symmetric papules/plaques with collarette of scale (Biett collarettes); anogenital condyloma lata; seborrheic area "corona veneris" along hairline · mucous membrane (30%) mucous patches · patchy alopecia (7%) "moth-eaten".
- p85 macular syphilis trunk (non-scaling oval pink ill-defined) · plantar lesions extending to lateral foot. p86 palms & soles (macular/papular, discrete/diffuse, nonscaling/slightly scaly/hyperkeratotic "syphilitic corn"). p87 moth-eaten alopecia (occipital; eyebrows/beard). p88 condyloma lata (vulva, perianal) — moist flat-topped papules.
- p89 Fig 82.11 cutaneous gummas of tertiary syphilis — arciform erythematous eroded plaques with central scarring.
- p90 chancre + Fig 82.3 positive darkfield — corkscrew spirochaete, deliberate forward/backward movement with rotation about longitudinal axis.

## p93–94 Chancroid
- p93 ✍️ "แผลริมอ่อน กดเจ็บ". Chancroid at a glance: STI acute ulcerative disease of anogenital area, often with inguinal adenitis/bubo · Haemophilus ducreyi — Gram-negative facultative anaerobic coccobacillus · still common in developing countries (Africa, Caribbean, SW Asia) · painful, soft ulcers with ragged undermined margins 1–2 weeks after inoculation (prepuce/frenulum in men; vulva, cervix, perianal in women) · nonsexual transmission reported · facilitates HIV transmission · culture problematic; DNA amplification more sensitive but not routine · azithromycin and ceftriaxone single-dose treatment. Photos: early sharply circumscribed ulcer coronal sulcus; vulvar chancroid with undermined edges.
- p94 Gram stain of exudate — chaining pattern ("school of fish") of H. ducreyi.

## p95–99 Gonorrhoea
- p95 key features: most common reportable STI in high-income countries · N. gonorrhoeae infects mucosa of genital tract, anus/rectum, mouth via direct (usually sexual) contact · cutaneous pustules + systemic (arthritis, fever) from haematogenous dissemination in a small % · incidence declined but recent increase in Eastern Europe · AMR increasing. Photo creamy purulent urethral discharge.
- p96 Gram stain urethral discharge: gonococci (Gram-negative diplococci) within PMN.
- p97 DGI (✍️ "GC", "หนองใน", "Disseminated gonococcal infection"): 0.5–3% of cases, with bacteraemia; classic triad (highlighted) dermatitis, migratory polyarthritis, tenosynovitis · true septic arthritis due to gonorrhoea more typically mono-/pauciarticular (highlighted), polyarticular more with active bacteraemia · skin: small–medium macules or most typically haemorrhagic vesicopustules on erythematous base on palms & soles or trunk/extremities; necrotic centres "gun metal gray"; tender or painless; cutaneous lesions in 40–70% of DGI · histology: perivascular neutrophilia, dermal vasculitis, epidermal neutrophil infiltration (highlighted). ✍️ on photo "ผื่นป้อนหนองตาม distal organ" (pustules on distal extremities).
