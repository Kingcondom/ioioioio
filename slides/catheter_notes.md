## Type selection (EAUN)
- Indwelling: consider other methods (male external catheter, intermittent) when appropriate — LE 1b, GR A
- Intermittent preferable to indwelling urethral or suprapubic in bladder emptying dysfunction if clinically appropriate & practical — 1b, B
- "AVOID using indwelling catheter as much as you can!" — to minimise CA-UTI

## Material
- (first slide, probably PVC/intermittent): cheap, durable, flexible · single use · may cause reaction
- Silicone: higher cost, durable, flexible · longer use (4–12 weeks) · hypoallergenic, biocompatible
- Latex: cheap, flexible, more discomfort · short-term (< 14 days) · ± irritation, allergy · "? More UTI"

## Fixation
- Good fixation & stabilisation reduces dislodgement, tissue trauma (necrosis), inflammation, UTI (pictures √ √ / √ X — image only)
- Men: secure to abdomen; women: to the leg (expert opinion ∂)

## Catheter care (EAUN)
- Hand hygiene immediately before/after any manipulation; disposable gloves — 1b B
- Maintain unobstructed flow; no kinking — 1b B
- Collecting bag below bladder level at all times; do not rest bag on floor — 1b B

## Troubleshooting
### Difficult to insert!! (male)
1. Directly inject sterile lubricant into urethra
2. Change angle of penis into 90°, ask patient to strain
3. Use other size of catheter (smaller in stricture / larger in BPH)
4. Consider Tiemann tip catheter
### Difficult to insert!! (female)
1. Insert finger into vaginal canal 2. Retry inserting the catheter above your finger
### Tiemann/Coudé catheter! (Campbell-Walsh-Wein Urology 12th ed 2021) — image
### Painful after indwelling catheter!! — Catheter-related bladder discomfort (CRBD), "25%" on slide
- Exclude other serious causes (CA-UTI, trauma, stone, debris) · treat constipation · remove catheter if not indicated · anticholinergic, intravesical botulinum toxin (EAUN 2024; BAUS-BAUN 2021)
### Hematuria after catheterization!! "25%"
- Usually self-limiting · initial: bladder irrigation with 3-way catheter · urology consult if persists/severe
- "!" NOT USE ANTIFIBRINOLYTIC THERAPY

### BAUS-BAUN 2021 misc problems (read from image)
(1) UTI during catheterization — inadequate asepsis/cleansing/contaminated tip → treat, correct technique · UTI via drainage system — breaking closed system, poor hand hygiene → bag position, hand hygiene · Recurrent CAUTI — colonisation inevitable after 4 weeks; stones/debris/poor hygiene → dipstick of CSU NOT recommended; prophylactic antibiotics usually ineffective; US/cystoscopy for stones, washout · Urethral mucosa trauma — wrong size/position/technique → recatheterise correct size; false passage → remove catheter if not draining, cystoscopy · Urethral erosion (catheter hypospadias) — chronic pressure → fixation, hygiene, consider SPC · Paraphimosis — failure to replace foreskin → retract for insertion and replace post catheterisation
(2) Artificial urinary sphincter in situ → deactivate/expert · Bladder spasm & pain — stones/debris → cystoscopy/US, washouts, anticholinergic/mirabegron/intravesical botox · Inadequate drainage — kinked/blocked (clots, debris)/incorrect placement → free flow, irrigation; temporarily holding bag above bladder releases suction effect (mucosa occludes eyelets); fluid intake, avoid constipation · Leakage around catheter (bypassing) — blocked lumen, eyehole blockage, mucosal oedema, bladder spasm/detrusor overactivity → support bag, bag below bladder, anticholinergic/mirabegron/botox; correct balloon size (no more, no less)
(3) Catheter falls out — incorrect balloon filling, bladder neck dilatation (women, neuropathic), deflated balloon → check water volume; larger catheter/balloon NOT recommended; consider SPC · Inability to deflate balloon → let valve deflate spontaneously with syringe attached; add 2 mL sterile water or air into inflation channel; syringe+needle aspirate from inflation arm; walk around; US-guided balloon puncture · Difficult catheter changes → SPC, coudé/Tiemann for enlarged prostate, exchange over guidewire
